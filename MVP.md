MVP Direction Paper  
The minimum viable product (MVP) is the concept that the early stages of a project created by a team can attract customers and create a product idea to be developed down the line. Teams would collect the minimum needed information from the customer and create a product that can attract and satisfy the customer. The primary benefit of this is the ability to gauge interest in a product, without using the time and resources to fully develop the project.  
The MVP of the project is currently not a “what if” since the project has been developed for several years now. It can be expected that the MVP will start from where my team is picking up from.  
Since we already have had our MVP up and running, we have been given useful information from a faculty member who acts as our more permanent product owner, Christine. A list of the feedback we have received includes criticisms on how the touchscreen functionality is not noticeable, how the professors aren’t automatically updated alongside their office numbers, no pictures of the professors, and the physical appearance of the billboard does not look very inviting. A list of further implementations that could improve the billboard is implementing a meeting function with the professors so that meetings can be scheduled, a campus map direction function that can point out where offices are located, accessibility functions for better visibility functions, or even text-to-speech functionality, and a feedback feature for everyday customers to leave.  
In the future, our work will consist of implementing as many of these ideas as possible and cleaning up the criticisms with an improved realization of what the product should look like. We plan to make it more user-friendly, accessible, and fully featured. Some improvement ideas we have so far include adding a “touch me” sign on or above the billboard to make its functionality as a guide apparent, making it look more kiosk-like by flipping the billboard, creating a web-scraper program that can automatically update professor’s information, make scheduling meetings with professors a possibility with the touch of a few buttons, and highlighting campus areas for showing directions. These are just a few implementations we’ll be able to work on in the future, however, as time goes on we will continue to gather feedback and progress our project accordingly. This will be more important for our newer members as the feedback we have been given will be able to point them in the right direction for what is most important to add to and change in our project.
